# WNBA 2026 Roster Value Framework + Data Schema

## Document Purpose

This document adapts the original 2025 class-project framework — **“Hidden Value Gems: High Impact, Low Cost Players”** — into a 2026-ready roster evaluation system for WNBA team-building, contract valuation, and hidden talent identification. 🏀📊✨

The original project evaluated individual undervalued contracts. This adapted version evaluates **full team rosters** by identifying undervalued, hidden-talent, fairly priced, overpriced, and strategically scarce players under the new 2026 WNBA contract environment. 📈🧠🏀

The 2026 context matters because the market changed all at once: ⚙️📉🚀

- New CBA salary structure
- Major increase in salary cap and player minimums
- Large free-agent reset
- Expansion pressure from Portland and Toronto
- Expansion draft plus regular draft roster reshuffling
- More teams competing for starter-quality and rotation-quality players
- Veteran guards, two-way wings, switchable forwards, and playable centers becoming harder to price cleanly

The goal is not just to ask, **“Who is cheap?”** 🤔💰📉

The better 2026 question is: 🎯📊🔥

> **Which players are being paid below the going rate for their production, role scarcity, playoff utility, age/experience profile, and team-building value?** 💡📉🏀

# DOC 1 — 2026 WNBA Roster Value Evaluation Framework

## 1. Main Narrative

## “Roster Value in a Reset Market: Finding Hidden Talent After the CBA Shock”

The 2026 WNBA season creates a new market environment. With higher salary minimums, higher maximum salaries, more cap room, new expansion teams, and widespread free agency, teams are no longer simply deciding who is “good” or “cheap.” They are deciding which player profiles are worth paying for in a reset market. 🔄💰📊

This framework evaluates each team roster through four connected lenses: 🧩📊🏀

### Thread 1 — Market Reset Value

Players are evaluated against the new 2026 salary environment rather than older salary baselines. 💰📉📊

Key questions: ❓📋🔍

- Is the player underpaid relative to 2026 positional market rates?
- Is the player’s cap hit low relative to comparable players?
- Is the player outperforming their salary tier?
- Is their current salary a true discount or just a product of the old contract cycle?

### Thread 2 — Role Scarcity

The 2026 market rewards scarce player profiles differently than past seasons. 🧠📉🔥

Scarce profiles may include: 📊🏀⚡

- Starter-level point-of-attack defenders
- Guards who defend, organize, and create
- Two-way wings
- Switchable forwards
- Centers who can defend in space
- Low-usage/high-efficiency connectors
- High-minute veterans with playoff experience
- Players who stabilize expansion or young rosters

### Thread 3 — Roster Construction Fit

A player’s value changes depending on team context. 🧩🏀📊

The same player may be: 🔄📈📉

- Undervalued on a contender
- Overextended on a rebuilding team
- A stabilizer for an expansion team
- A luxury on a roster already deep at that archetype
- More valuable if their skill set fills a team’s distribution gap

### Thread 4 — Pricing-Out Risk

Because salary floors and minimums rose sharply, some middle-tier veterans and narrow-skill role players may face new pressure. ⚠️💰📉

Key question: 🎯📊❗

> **Did the new salary structure make some players too expensive relative to roster spots, role scarcity, and replacement options?** 🧠📉💸

This is where the 2026 framework becomes more front-office relevant than the original project. It is no longer just a hidden-gems analysis. It becomes a **cap strategy, market demand, and roster distribution analysis**. 🏀📊🔥

# 5. Core Definition: What Makes a Player Undervalued in 2026?

A player is classified as **undervalued** when they are paid below the 2026 market rate for their role, production, scarcity, and team-building value. 📉💰🏀

In 2026, the definition needs to be stricter than the original 2025 class project. ⚖️📊🧠

## Original 2025 Definition

A player was undervalued if they had: 📋📉🏀

- Salary below position average or below $100K
- TS% above 70th percentile
- 2+ hidden value strengths
- 10+ MPG
- Positive comparable salary discount

## Adapted 2026 Definition

A player is undervalued if they meet most or all of the following criteria: 📊🧠🔥

### Criterion 1 — Below-Market Salary Relative to 2026 Contract Environment

A player qualifies financially if: 💰📉📊

- Salary is below the average for their position/archetype, or
- Salary is below comparable players with similar production, or
- Salary is low relative to their projected role, or
- Salary occupies a lower tier than their performance profile suggests

Recommended measures: 📊📈🧠

- Salary percentile by position
- Salary percentile by archetype
- Salary percentile by age band
- Salary percentile by experience band
- Salary as % of team cap
- Salary vs comparable player average
- Salary vs expected salary model
- Dollar discount vs comps
- Percent discount vs comps

### Criterion 2 — Position-Relative Production Strength

A player should show strong production relative to position. 📊🏀📈

Recommended thresholds: 📈📋🎯

- 70th+ percentile in at least one primary skill area
- 60th+ percentile in multiple secondary skill areas
- Positive role-adjusted impact
- Production that holds up after minutes and usage context

Important metrics: 📊🏀🔍

- TS%
- eFG%
- Usage rate
- Assist rate
- Turnover rate
- Assist-to-turnover ratio
- Rebound rate
- Steal rate
- Block rate
- Free throw rate
- On/off net rating
- Offensive rating differential
- Defensive rating differential
- Lineup impact

### Criterion 3 — Role Scarcity

A player receives additional value if their skill set is scarce. 🧠🔥📊

Potential scarce 2026 profiles: 📋🏀⚡

- Veteran lead guard
- Defensive organizer
- Point-of-attack defender
- Two-way wing
- Stretch forward
- Switchable big
- Low-usage connector
- High-efficiency bench scorer
- Rotation player with playoff experience
- Expansion-roster stabilizer

### Criterion 4 — Hidden Value Strengths

A player should have at least **2 hidden value strengths**. 🔍📊✨

Hidden value categories: 📋🧠⚡

1. **Defensive disruption**
   - Steals percentile
   - Blocks percentile
   - Defensive event rate
   - Matchup difficulty

2. **Playmaking stability**
   - Assist rate
   - Assist-to-turnover ratio
   - Turnover suppression
   - On/off team turnover impact

3. **Floor spacing impact**
   - Team 3PT% with player on court
   - 3PA rate impact
   - Catch-and-shoot gravity

4. **Possession creation**
   - Offensive rebounds
   - Deflections
   - Loose balls
   - Steals

5. **Lineup portability**
   - Performs across multiple lineup types
   - Fits with stars
   - Can play in different pace/spacing contexts

6. **Playoff utility**
   - Defensive versatility
   - Low-mistake decision-making
   - Experience in high-leverage games
   - Can stay on floor in matchup basketball

7. **Scalability**
   - Efficient in low/moderate usage
   - Production projects upward with more minutes
   - Does not require high usage to create value

### Criterion 5 — Role-to-Salary Alignment

A player’s salary should match or understate their role. ⚖️💰📊

Example logic: 📋🔍📉

- Starter production + rotation salary = undervalued
- Rotation production + minimum salary = undervalued
- Bench production + starter salary = overvalued risk
- Specialist production + premium salary = context dependent
- Veteran stabilizer + mid-tier salary = fair/undervalued if role scarcity is high

### Criterion 6 — Risk-Adjusted Value

A player’s value grade should include risk. ⚠️📉🧠

Risk factors: 📋🔍⚡

- Age curve decline
- Injury history
- Low minutes sample
- Efficiency regression risk
- Usage scalability risk
- Defensive matchup limitations
- Limited playoff viability
- Contract length risk
- Roster redundancy

# 6. Roster-Level Evaluation Framework

This version should evaluate both players and teams. 🧩🏀📊

## Team Roster Value Grade

Each team receives a grade from A to F based on five dimensions. 📊🧠🏀

### Dimension 1 — Cap Efficiency

Measures how well the team converts salary into production. 💰📈📊

Inputs: 📋🔍⚡

- Team salary used
- Salary as % of cap
- Player value above expected salary
- Number of undervalued players
- Number of overpriced contracts
- Production per cap dollar

Suggested weight: **25%**

### Dimension 2 — Role Balance

Measures whether the roster has enough functional role coverage. 🧩🏀📊

Inputs: 📋🔍⚡

- Lead guards
- Secondary creators
- Wings
- Forwards
- Centers
- Defensive specialists
- Floor spacers
- Connectors
- High-usage scorers
- Low-usage efficiency players

Suggested weight: **20%**

### Dimension 3 — Age/Experience Curve

Measures whether the roster has a healthy mix of development, prime, and veteran stability. 📈📊🧠

Suggested age bands: 📋🔍⚡

- 18–23: development/rookie curve
- 24–27: early prime
- 28–31: prime/established veteran
- 32–35: veteran stability/risk band
- 36+: late-career/high-risk veteran band

Suggested weight: **15%**

### Dimension 4 — Scarcity Coverage

Measures whether the team has scarce player types that are hard to replace. 🧠🔥📊

Examples: 📋🏀⚡

- Point-of-attack defender
- Playmaking guard
- Two-way wing
- Switchable forward
- Rim protector
- Stretch big
- Low-turnover organizer

Suggested weight: **20%**

### Dimension 5 — Playoff/High-Leverage Utility

Measures whether the roster has players who can survive playoff matchup conditions. 🏀🔥📊

Inputs: 📋🔍⚡

- Playoff games played
- Playoff minutes
- Defensive versatility
- Turnover control
- Shooting reliability
- Star compatibility
- Closing lineup viability

Suggested weight: **20%**

# 7. Player Value Score

Create a 0–100 player score. 📊🏀📈

## Recommended Formula

```text
Player Value Score =
  25% Production Percentile Score
+ 20% Salary Value Score
+ 15% Role Scarcity Score
+ 15% Hidden Impact Score
+ 10% Scalability Score
+ 10% Playoff Utility Score
+ 5% Risk Adjustment
```

## Score Bands

| Score | Label | Meaning |
|---:|---|---|
| 90–100 | Elite Value | Star/near-star value below market |
| 80–89 | Strong Undervalued | Clear positive value contract |
| 70–79 | Hidden Talent | Strong role-adjusted value, may need context |
| 60–69 | Fair Value | Salary mostly matches profile |
| 50–59 | Fragile Value | Context-dependent, risk present |
| <50 | Overpriced Risk | Salary likely exceeds role/production |

- Age-band average salary

## Suggested Formula

```text
Salary Value Score =
  40% Comparable Salary Discount
+  25% Position Salary Discount
+  20% Archetype Salary Discount
+  15% Cap Efficiency
```

## Salary Classification

| Classification | Condition |
|---|---|
| Undervalued | Salary is 15%+ below comparable market |
| Fair Value | Salary is within +/- 15% of comparable market |
| Overvalued | Salary is 15%+ above comparable market |
| Severe Overpay Risk | Salary is 30%+ above comparable market |

# 12. Pricing-Out Analysis

## Core Question

> **Did the major rise in salary minimums and team caps price out certain types of players?** 💰⚠️📉

This should be treated as a hypothesis, not assumed. 🧠📊⚖️

## Player Types Potentially at Risk

- Older veterans without clear starter roles
- Low-efficiency scorers
- One-way specialists
- Low-minute bench players
- Players without defensive versatility
- Players without shooting gravity
- Replacement-level players whose minimum salary is now much higher

## Player Types Potentially Helped

- Stars and superstars
- High-end starters
- Scarce role players
- Veteran leaders on young/expansion teams
- Rookies if salary scale is still efficient relative to production
- Two-way wings and defensive guards

## Pricing-Out Indicators

A player archetype may be getting priced out if: 📉🔍📊

- Fewer players from that archetype appear on 2026 rosters
- Their average salary does not rise as much as the market average
- Similar players remain unsigned longer
- Teams replace them with rookies or younger players
- Their role disappears from roster construction patterns
- They are selected less often in expansion/free agency despite availability

- 2024–2026 salary tier distribution
- Age-band roster share over time
- Veteran role-player salary vs roster share
- Replacement-level salary floor trend
- Signed vs unsigned by age/archetype
- Salary compression chart

> All percentiles currently computed by `build_doc2_roster_tables.py` are **league-wide** (all players in the same season/season_type group), even though column names carry a `_pos` suffix (e.g., `ts_pctile_pos`, `ast_pctile_pos`). This naming is a historical artefact — the suffix does not indicate position-filtered ranking.
>
> **Planned enhancement:** Position-specific percentiles (Guard vs Forward vs Center vs Wing pools) are the correct frame for evaluating production relative to peers at the same role. Until implemented, case studies should:
> 1. Report league-wide percentile as the primary figure.
> 2. Accompany it with a note that guards have a structurally higher AST% baseline than forwards, which inflates a guard's league-wide AST% rank relative to a position-matched rank.
> 3. Use position_group from `player_bios_2026.csv` or `player_archetypes.csv` to filter the percentile pool manually when position-specific comparisons are needed.
>
> **Multi-year rate stats:** `player_archetypes.csv` now includes `ast_pct_yr2023`, `ast_pct_yr2024`, `ast_pct_yr2025`, `ts_pct_yr{year}`, `usage_pct_yr{year}`, and `ast_pct_trend_dir` (up/stable/down) for all players with Regular Season data in that year. Case studies should display the 3-season progression for AST%, TS%, and usage rather than the single-season snapshot.
