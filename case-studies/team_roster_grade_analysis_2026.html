1. Method snapshot

One calculation path for the ranking, visuals, and write-up

2026 local build

The deliverable reads the updated team_roster_grades, value_scores, team_rosters, and team_salary_cap tables, then rebuilds the team view directly from those files. That keeps the HTML narrative aligned with the same tables you are updating elsewhere in the project.

  • Team grades are rebuilt from the 2026 local value-score table and sorted by median player value score, which keeps the grade focused on the typical roster slot instead of one star contract.
  • Cap concentration is recalculated as the share of total cap hit carried by each team’s top two salaries, using live salary rows with the team salary-cap table as the denominator.
  • Roster tiers, undervalued counts, and hidden-value counts are all derived from the same fresh player universe so every chart and takeaway stays on one calculation path.
  • Saved Tableau extracts are loaded only as a comparison check. If they drift materially from the fresh analysis, this notebook does not inherit those rankings.

2. Team Value Leaderboard

The cleanest read is still the middle roster slot

The leaderboard is sorted by median player value score. That makes it a roster-quality chart first, rather than a star-power chart. Hidden-value counts sit to the right as a quick read on whether each team is also finding cheap excess value.

3. Cap Concentration vs. Team Value

High-value teams are not all built the same way

The scatter separates teams by how much of the cap sits in the top two contracts and whether the median roster slot still grades well. It is a quick way to isolate star-dependent builds from teams getting similar quality with a more distributed payroll.

4. Roster Tier Stacked Bar by Team

Depth quality becomes visible when every roster is broken into the same value tiers

This view keeps the leaderboard order but replaces the single team score with a full roster composition read. Teams with a broader fair-to-strong middle look sturdier than clubs that rely on one or two acceptable contracts while the rest of the roster sits in the risk band.

5. Hidden Value Talent by Team

Cheap surplus talent is still the fastest leverage story in the model

The dot plot isolates which teams are turning low-cost contracts into real value. Dot size adds roster size context so a team with the same hidden-value count but a smaller roster reads as a more concentrated efficiency win.
